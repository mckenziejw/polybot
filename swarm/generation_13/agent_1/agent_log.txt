## Results Summary

**Strategy: Down-Only Deep Imbalance (Bid-Heavy Deep Layering)**

The original hypothesis (ask-side deep_level_ratio alone) was essentially flat (~50.7% WR). However, during analysis I discovered that the **imbalance between ask and bid deep layering** is the real signal:

**Signal:** `deep_imbalance = ask_deep_ratio - bid_deep_ratio`
When negative (bid side has more deep liquidity), buy Down.

### Primary results (with spread filter, threshold -0.30):
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Trades | 26 |
| Win Rate | **73.1%** |
| PnL/trade | **+0.2196** |
| Total PnL | +5.71 |
| Sharpe | 8.33 |
| Max DD | -1.02 |

### Without spread filter (more trades):
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Trades | 124 |
| Win Rate | **60.5%** |
| PnL/trade | **+0.1054** |
| Total PnL | +13.07 |
| Sharpe | 3.66 |

### Key findings:
1. The signal is **monotonically increasing** — more negative imbalance = higher WR, consistently across all threshold/spread combinations
2. The spread filter adds significant alpha (60.5% → 73.1% WR), suggesting the signal works best in less liquid markets
3. Even at the loosest threshold (imbal <= 0.0), the spread-filtered variant shows 61.5% WR

**Verdict: Promising** — needs validation on the full 7k+ market dataset to confirm the edge isn't sample-specific, but the monotonic signal structure and robustness across parameters is encouraging.
